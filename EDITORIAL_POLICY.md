# Editorial Policy

## Principle

Accuracy, attribution and provenance take priority over speed, engagement and automation.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer primary evidence: official announcements, regulator/government publications, exchange filings, original research and first-party documentation. Secondary journalism provides corroboration and context. Source authority is editable by humans and is not silently overwritten by AI.

## Claim-level verification

Important factual statements are stored as atomic claims with supporting/contradicting evidence. Unverified, rejected or conflicted claims cannot silently become publishable facts. Article citations derive from verified evidence.

## AI use

AI may assist extraction, entity/claim structuring, drafting, SEO variants, summaries and grounded Q&A. It may not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates, motives or predictions. Source content is untrusted data. Deterministic gates and editors retain consequential control.

## High-risk coverage

Politics/elections, public policy, legal allegations, health/medical claims, high-impact financial claims and major cybersecurity incidents require human review. Political/electoral coverage must be neutral and factual, attribute contested claims, avoid endorsements/persuasion/rankings and not generate election-winner predictions.

## Corrections

Significant factual errors are corrected through a versioned correction workflow. The reason, relevant previous/corrected value and publication time remain visible rather than being silently overwritten.

## Automation

Initial publication is human-controlled. Shadow autonomy records what the system would have done. Controlled auto-publishing is only eligible after explicit policy activation and all deterministic gates pass; high-risk categories remain human-reviewed.
